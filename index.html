Pivot: From Startups to Enterprise Innovation

Canada's biggest innovation opportunity may be inside its largest companies.
My Journey

I believed innovation happened mostly in startups

  • Grew up in the Kitchener-Waterloo startup ecosystem
  • Joined D2L early. Designer → Product Manager. Fast-growing, watched it scale, get mature.
  • Tulip. New and exciting product segment. Developer → Manager → VP.
  • Then I wanted to swing for the C-level fences!
So I joined a venture-backed startup: ShipperBee 🐝
The Moonshot

What if we did to last-mile delivery what Uber did to taxis?

The vision
  • Crowdsourced labor
  • Unused vehicle capacity
  • Parcel lockers along commuting corridors
  • Technology-first logistics
  • It felt inevitable!
Reality hit hard
  • Every "touch" costs money. We built nothing but touches
  • Frozen goods couldn't sit in a stranger's car
  • Complexity grew faster than volume
  • The technology was hard. The business model was harder.
#startuplife — 5:00am production issues. Two kids, a third on the way, a new mortgage, not good.
The Call
The CEO called. We were shutting down. My job was to keep it together as we sold for parts.

Purolator bought some of the technology, and with it, the innovative people.

That team became the Digital Lab. I'd landed inside a large Canadian enterprise.
The Discovery

Innovation opportunities we hunted were everywhere in enterprise!

Large companies have real ...
Customers
Revenue

Most Importantly
  • Thousands of people with real, well-understood problems
If enterprises have the raw ingredients of innovation, why is innovative output so ... inconsistent?
Before We Get Into It, Caveat

Enterprises move carefully for good reasons

Caution is Good
  • Real customers don't want to absorb failed innovation experiments
  • Regulatory, legal, safety obligations compund
  • Thousands of jobs attached to predictable execution
  • Rational tradeoffs, and well studied — I'm not here with a new theory
Today's question isn't why enterprises struggle with innovation, it's how to help it along. I've watched that up close.

Core Idea #1

Fund Innovation from the Top

Leadership Support
  • Executives don't lose imagination, as much gain accountability
  • Predictable execution trumps experimentation
  • Everyone aligns that innovation matters. But resourcing, prioritizing is tricky
Exec Ownership + Strategic Alignment
  • Digital Lab reports to the CTO (rather a random manager or director)
  • Use the language of the organization, tie to strategy
  • Funding considers revenue, savings, opportunity cost
  • Sought advice from Lab leaders (Canadian Tire, Rogers, Wawanesa, others)
Innovation survives when it's strategically aligned + budgeted. C-Levels provide both.
Core Idea #2

Empower the multi-functional, cross-team innovator

  • Innovation often happens at intersections. Enterprises have many departments to intersect.
  • Tech teams don't have a monopoly on innovation (but core enabler).
Robotics + Facilities (ex. Autonomous Cleaning)
Finance + Technology (ex. Pricing Flexibility)
Marketing + Operations (ex. Red Bag Campaign)
Robotics leader

Not an engineer, 25 years in terminal operations. Relationships and insight mattered more than the eng credentials. Now running humanoids.

Software leader

Despite day job, biggest impact is in Retail operations. In-store experience is what he cares deeply about.

You may not need a Lab? Cross-functional skunkworks drive good, innovative value.
Core Idea #3

Don't outsource all your innovation to others

Normal Purchase Cycle
  • Problem → vendor → exciting demo → RFP
  • Defines the problem + solution with their hot PPTX deck.
  • Vendors know their products and market, you know your opportunity and tradeoffs.
Own Discovery Results
  • Even if you're not an eng/tech org, you can do: needs assessment, experimentation, business case, validation, design excercises
  • I was overconfident in the Lab's capability, while the enterprise was overly skeptical. The truth was in the middle.
Labs are good at supporting + capturing innovation, even if they don't build it all.
Closing Thoughts
Canada doesn't only need more entrepreneurs, it needs innovators inside its enterprises.

We have the talent, customers, revenue, opportunities. We need people to transform our large institutions to innovate actively.
